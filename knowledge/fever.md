# Managing a fever

## What a fever is
A fever is a temporary rise in body temperature, usually a sign that the body is fighting an infection. In adults a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher is generally considered a fever.

## Caring for yourself at home
Rest, drink plenty of fluids such as water, oral rehydration solution or soup, and wear light clothing. Keep the room comfortably cool. A lukewarm sponge bath can help you feel more comfortable; avoid cold baths or ice. Fever-reducing medicines are available from pharmacies — follow the label or your doctor's or pharmacist's advice, and do not combine several products without asking.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if a fever lasts more than three days, keeps returning, or is very high (above 39.5°C / 103°F). Seek advice sooner for people with long-term conditions, pregnancy, or a weak immune system.

## Fever in babies and children
Any fever in a baby younger than three months needs urgent medical assessment. For older children, see a doctor if the fever lasts more than a day or two, the child is unusually drowsy, refuses to drink, or is not passing urine normally.

## Warning signs — get urgent care
Go to an emergency department or call 1122 for a fever with a stiff neck, severe headache, confusion, trouble breathing, a rash that does not fade when pressed, a seizure, or persistent vomiting.
