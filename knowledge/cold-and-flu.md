# Common cold and flu

## Cold or flu?
Both are viral infections of the nose, throat and chest. A cold usually starts gradually with a runny or blocked nose, sneezing and a sore throat. Flu tends to start suddenly with fever, body aches, headache and marked tiredness, and is usually more severe.

## Looking after yourself
Rest, drink plenty of fluids, and use saline nose drops or steam to ease congestion. Gargling warm salt water can soothe a sore throat. Antibiotics do not work against viruses and should only be taken if a doctor prescribes them.

## Preventing spread
Wash your hands often, cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or your elbow, stay home when you are unwell, and avoid sharing cups and towels. A yearly flu vaccine is recommended for many people, especially older adults, pregnant women and people with chronic conditions — ask your doctor.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if symptoms last more than ten days, get worse after improving, you have a high fever that persists, ear or sinus pain, or you have asthma, diabetes, heart disease or a weak immune system.

## Warning signs — get urgent care
Seek urgent help for difficulty breathing, chest pain, blue lips, confusion, or being unable to keep fluids down.
