# Diabetes basics

## What it is
Diabetes is a long-term condition in which the body cannot keep blood sugar (glucose) at healthy levels. Type 1 diabetes happens when the body makes little or no insulin. Type 2 diabetes, the most common type, develops when the body does not use insulin well; it is linked to weight, inactivity and family history.

## Common symptoms
Frequent urination, strong thirst, constant hunger, tiredness, blurred vision, slow-healing cuts and unexplained weight loss. Some people have no symptoms for years, so people with risk factors should be screened.

## Day-to-day management
Follow the eating plan your doctor or dietitian recommends, favouring whole grains, vegetables and protein and limiting sugary drinks and sweets. Be active regularly, take medicines or insulin exactly as prescribed, and monitor blood sugar if advised. Attend regular check-ups, including eye, kidney and foot checks.

## Foot care
Check your feet daily for cuts, blisters or sores, wash and dry them carefully, wear well-fitting shoes, and see a doctor promptly about any wound that does not heal.

## When to seek urgent care
Seek urgent medical help for very high blood sugar with vomiting, stomach pain, fast breathing or confusion, or for low blood sugar with shakiness, sweating, confusion or fainting that does not improve after the treatment your doctor has advised.
