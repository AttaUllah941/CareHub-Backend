# High blood pressure (hypertension) basics

## What it is
High blood pressure means the force of blood against your artery walls is consistently too high. It often has no symptoms, which is why regular checks matter. Over time it increases the risk of heart attack, stroke and kidney problems.

## Measuring it
Blood pressure is written as two numbers, for example 120/80 mmHg. A diagnosis is made by a doctor using several readings taken on different occasions, not from a single high reading. Rest for five minutes, sit with your back supported and your arm at heart level before measuring at home.

## Lifestyle steps that help
Eat less salt and fewer processed or fried foods, include more vegetables, fruit and whole grains, stay physically active most days, keep a healthy weight, limit tea and coffee if they raise your readings, and do not smoke. Managing stress and sleeping well also help.

## Medicines and follow-up
If medicine is prescribed, take it exactly as directed and do not stop it because you feel well. Keep regular appointments so your doctor can check your readings and adjust treatment.

## When to seek urgent care
Call 1122 or go to an emergency department for a very high reading together with chest pain, severe headache, shortness of breath, confusion, weakness on one side of the body, or trouble speaking.
