# Headaches and migraine

## Common types
Tension headaches feel like a tight band or pressure on both sides of the head and are often linked to stress, poor sleep or long hours at a screen. Migraines usually cause a throbbing pain on one side, often with nausea and sensitivity to light or sound, and can last hours to days.

## What can help
Rest in a quiet, dark room, drink water, eat regular meals, and keep a regular sleep routine. Note possible triggers such as skipped meals, lack of sleep, stress, strong smells or certain foods. Over-the-counter pain relievers should be used as directed on the label or by a pharmacist; using them too often can make headaches worse.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if headaches are frequent, keep getting worse, wake you from sleep, or change in pattern, or if you need pain relievers on most days.

## Warning signs — get urgent care
Seek emergency care for a sudden, severe "worst ever" headache, a headache after a head injury, or a headache with fever and stiff neck, confusion, weakness, vision changes, trouble speaking or a seizure.
