# Diarrhea, vomiting and dehydration

## Why it matters
Diarrhea and vomiting are usually caused by infections or food poisoning and often settle within a few days. The main danger is dehydration, especially in babies, young children, older adults and pregnant women.

## What to do
Keep drinking small, frequent sips of oral rehydration solution (ORS), which replaces water and salts. Continue to eat light foods such as rice, bananas, toast or soup as soon as you can. Avoid very sugary drinks. Wash your hands carefully with soap, and drink only safe, boiled or bottled water.

## Signs of dehydration
Dry mouth, very little or dark urine, dizziness, sunken eyes, unusual sleepiness and, in babies, no wet nappy for several hours or a sunken soft spot on the head.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if diarrhea lasts more than two days, there is blood or black stool, you have a high fever, you cannot keep fluids down, or symptoms are severe or are in an infant or older adult.

## Urgent care
Go to an emergency department for signs of severe dehydration, confusion, severe stomach pain, or inability to drink.
